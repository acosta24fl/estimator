How to read this

Each row answers one question: if I look at the screen again in 5 minutes / an hour / a day, where is price likely to be?

The answer comes from history, not from an opinion. The system finds every past moment when the market looked like it does now — same trend direction, same trend strength, same level of volatility — and asks what happened next. The Likely price column is the middle of those outcomes: half the time it ended up higher, half the time lower.

The bar shows how spread out those outcomes were. The solid block is where price landed half the time. The faint block stretches out to the unusual cases — 8 out of 10 landed inside it. The vertical notch is the likely price, and the thin centre line is where price is right now. A wide bar means "genuinely could go either way", and that is information, not a flaw.

The last column is the one that matters most. USABLE means the system checked itself on data it had never seen and this row beat simply guessing "price stays where it is". JUST NOISE means it did not — the row is still shown for completeness, but you should ignore it. Most of the time most rows say JUST NOISE. That is the honest state of a market this efficient, and a panel claiming otherwise would be lying to you.

About this log

Every check, every score and every decision — including all the times it decided not to trade — is written to artifacts/decisions.jsonl as it happens. This panel shows the most recent entries.

The rejections are the valuable part. "Why did it skip that move?" is the question that improves the system, and it can only be answered from a written record of the skips.

What the four parts mean

Skill — how much better than guessing "no change" the best horizon is, measured on unseen data. This is the gate: at zero, the whole score is zero no matter how good the rest looks.

Conviction — how far the up/down call sits from a coin flip.

Payoff — whether the expected move is big enough to be worth the cost of getting in and out (about 0.9 points round trip on MNQ).

Agreement — whether the time horizons point the same way. Disagreement is a warning, not a detail.
